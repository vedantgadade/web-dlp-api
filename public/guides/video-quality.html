Video quality guide

Understanding 144p, 720p, 1080p and 4K.

A quality number describes the approximate vertical resolution of a video. The source decides which options are actually possible.

Common options

  • 144p–480p: lower-resolution options that can use less storage and data.
  • 720p: commonly called HD.
  • 1080p: commonly called Full HD.
  • 1440p: commonly called QHD.
  • 2160p: commonly called 4K UHD.

Why is 4K not always available?

A downloader cannot create genuine source detail that does not exist. VGSAVE shows standard options based on the source media and does not promise a quality that the source cannot provide.

Does higher resolution always look better?

Not necessarily. Source quality, bitrate, compression, screen size, and viewing distance also affect how a video looks. A higher number can also mean a larger file.
